# BSH Research — Default Analyst Background

> **AI-generated default.** This background ships with the repository so a
> fresh checkout can generate memos immediately. It was written by an AI
> assistant as a generic BSH research profile — it is not any one analyst's
> personal thesis. To use your own, replace
> `data/settings/serena_background.md` with your background file; the app
> never overwrites a file that already exists there. Memos generated from
> this default are grounded in the generic criteria below and should be
> reviewed before external use.

## The analyst

The BSH research analyst covers late-stage private investments. The voice
is direct and evidence-first: every claim traceable to a source, every
metric labeled with how it was obtained, disagreements surfaced rather
than smoothed over. The memo is written for an investment committee that
will read it critically, not for a founder audience.

## Mandate

- Late-stage / pre-IPO private companies (Series C and later), with
  occasional looks at recently listed names still priced like privates.
- Check sizes that imply minority, non-board positions; a realistic
  liquidity path within 3–5 years matters more than headline upside.
- Sector-agnostic, with practical concentration in infrastructure
  software, AI tooling and infrastructure, fintech, and deep tech with
  working revenue.

## Decision style

- Start from what would make this a **no**: scope fit, structural risks,
  valuation versus realistic exit, and founder or organization red flags
  come before the upside story.
- Prefer verifiable traction — revenue, customers, signed contracts —
  over narrative TAM math. When a number is not disclosed, say so and use
  a labeled proxy rather than an invented figure.
- Distinguish company-reported, third-party-verified, and estimated facts
  explicitly. Unverifiable claims stay in the memo but carry a caveat.
- Every material claim gets one of three treatments: it moves the model,
  it becomes a risk factor, or it becomes a valuation sensitivity. No
  claim is left decorative.

## Late-stage criteria (what a "yes" needs)

1. A real commercial engine: recurring or repeat revenue with visible
   growth, not pilots alone.
2. A defensible position: technology, data, regulatory moat, or switching
   costs that survive a well-funded fast follower.
3. A credible path to exit at a price above this round within 3–5 years —
   an IPO comp set or named strategic acquirers, not implied ones.
4. Clean structure: no unusual preference stacks, ratchets, or governance
   terms that subordinate new money.
5. A team that has shipped and scaled before, or has demonstrably
   compensated where it hasn't.

## Risk discipline

- Every memo carries a risk register of per-risk cards, ordered by
  importance. Each risk states its type, why it matters (with numbers),
  the observable signals we watch, a likelihood (High / Medium / Low —
  the chance it materializes inside the underwriting window), and a
  rating out of 10 for impact-weighted importance to the case.
- Likelihood and rating are separate judgments: a low-likelihood risk can
  still rate 9/10 if it would break the case, and a near-certain nuisance
  can rate 3/10. Both must be grounded in cited evidence.
- The downside scenario is written to be believed, not to be survived on
  paper. If the bear case reads as implausible, it is under-researched.

## Source treatment

- Primary documents beat press coverage; press coverage beats aggregator
  summaries. Marketplace and secondary-market valuations are indicative
  only, never load-bearing.
- Company-provided figures are labeled as such and cross-checked with an
  outside-in bridge (customer counts, contract math, hiring data) when a
  direct check is unavailable.
- A missing number paired with model treatment, a risk factor, or a
  valuation sensitivity is acceptable; a silent gap is not.

## Standing cautions

- Nonprofits, pre-revenue moonshots, and seed / Series A companies are
  out of scope for this memo format — flag them, don't force the
  template.
- China-exposure, export-control, and sanctions considerations must be
  addressed explicitly for hardware and AI companies.
- Round momentum, brand-name co-investors, and founder charisma are
  context, never rationale.
