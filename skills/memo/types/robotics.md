## What this type is
Companies that sell physical machines or the intelligence that runs them —
humanoids, dexterous hands, warehouse picking, mobile manipulation, and the
robot foundation models and sensing stacks beneath them. The case turns on
whether machines are working in customer sites at a unit margin, not on
demonstrations.

## Where the case usually lives
- **Business model and unit economics** — gross margin per unit after BOM
  and service; the BOM cost curve with volume; pricing (unit sale versus
  robots-as-a-service) and payback for the customer.
- **Market size and growth** — the labor pool in the target tasks is
  enormous; the honest number is the share reachable at today's
  capability and cost, with a stated path.
- **Team and governance** — manufacturing, supply-chain and safety
  leadership matter as much as research; capital discipline decides
  whether the company reaches volume.
- **Moat** — the deployment data flywheel (only if measurable), hardware
  cost leadership, and customer integration; say which is evidenced.

## Type-specific metrics the sections must carry
Units installed and operating (by customer, with dates) versus announced;
uptime and tasks per hour; BOM cost and trend; price per unit or per hour;
gross margin per unit; service cost; backlog and orders; pilot-to-fleet
conversion; cash runway to positive unit margin; capex for factory or fleet.

## Failure modes -> risk areas
Commercialization: announced logos never become installed units; pilots
stall for years. Technology: reliability and safety in unstructured
environments; sim-to-real gaps. Competition: Chinese hardware at a fraction
of the BOM; incumbents in structured tasks. Concentration: one anchor
customer or one investor-partner supplies the demand story. Valuation &
exit: the price assumes volume economics before a single fleet order.

## Comparables and exit norms
Industrial and warehouse automation peers (EV / revenue, EV / gross
profit), other humanoid and robot-model labs' private marks; exits are
strategic acquisitions by automotive, logistics or industrial groups, or a
late IPO once unit margins and fleet orders are public.
