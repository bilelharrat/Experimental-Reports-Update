## What this type is
Companies where generated video is the product or the production method —
video foundation models, creation tools, and short-drama or social-content
studios that generate, distribute and monetize serialized vertical video.
The economics are media economics with software costs: hits, retention, and
distribution take rates decide the outcome.

## Where the case usually lives
- **Revenue growth and quality** — revenue per title or per user, hit rate,
  repeat purchase and subscription retention; growth that comes from more
  titles at the same economics rather than one hit.
- **Business model and unit economics** — cost per finished minute, gross
  margin per title after platform take rates and creator payouts, and
  payback on content and acquisition spend.
- **Industry position** — an owned audience or IP library versus renting an
  audience from platforms; a model that leads on quality-per-dollar.
- **Market size and growth** — short drama is a multi-billion market in
  China already and growing internationally; the addressable share for
  generated content depends on quality thresholds audiences accept.

## Type-specific metrics the sections must carry
Cost per finished minute (and trend by model generation); titles released
and revenue by title (hit rate); paying users, ARPU and retention; platform
take rates and the share of revenue from platforms the company does not
own; creator payouts; model dependence (own / licensed / API, and cost per
minute of each); IP and copyright status of the catalog.

## Failure modes -> risk areas
Technology: a better third-party model erases a production-cost advantage
in one release. Concentration: one distribution platform carries the
audience and can change take rates or policy. Commercialization: hit-driven
revenue does not repeat; acquisition spend outruns lifetime value. Team,
governance & regulation: copyright and likeness claims; content regulation
in China and elsewhere. Valuation & exit: the price assumes software
multiples on media economics.

## Comparables and exit norms
Media and creator-economy peers (EV / revenue with margin adjustments),
short-drama platforms, and video-model labs' private marks; exits are
acquisitions by platforms or studios, or an IPO for a company with an owned
audience and repeatable title economics.
