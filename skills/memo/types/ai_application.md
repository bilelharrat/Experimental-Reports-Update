## What this type is
Products that package model capability into a workflow customers pay for —
legal drafting, enterprise knowledge, sales, support, coding, clinical
documentation. The model is rented; the value is the workflow, the data,
and the distribution.

## Where the case usually lives
- **Industry position** — category leadership in one vertical (revenue,
  logos, win rate against the two nearest rivals), not breadth.
- **Revenue growth and quality** — ARR growth with net revenue retention
  above 110% and pilot-to-production conversion; usage-based revenue that
  grows with adoption rather than seats that plateau.
- **Moat** — proprietary data and permissions layers, integrations into
  the systems customers already run, and switching costs created by
  workflow habit; the "context layer" a model vendor cannot see.
- **Business model and unit economics** — gross margin after model costs
  (60% is a warning, 80% is software), CAC payback, and the direction of
  revenue per customer as pricing moves from seats to outcomes.

## Type-specific metrics the sections must carry
ARR and growth; net and gross revenue retention; customers by segment and
the $1M+ cohort; pilot-to-production conversion; gross margin after model
costs; CAC payback; win rate versus named rivals; model-vendor dependence
(share of COGS, contract terms, fallback vendors).

## Failure modes -> risk areas
Competition: the model vendor ships the feature; the vertical incumbent
bundles it. Commercialization: pilots do not convert; seats shrink as
agents replace users. Technology: a cheaper model makes the product a
thin wrapper. Concentration: a few enterprise logos carry revenue.
Valuation & exit: the price assumes a category-leader multiple before the
category has a leader.

## Comparables and exit norms
Vertical SaaS and application peers on EV / forward ARR (growth-adjusted);
exits are strategic acquisitions by incumbents or model vendors, or an IPO
above roughly $300M ARR with net retention above 110%.
